Sloped areas need a little more planning than flat lawns. The main problem is that water tends to run off quickly, which can deprive parts of the slope of the moisture they need during the first few weeks.

For sloped ground, it is important to prepare the base carefully and avoid creating a smooth surface that lets water slide away too fast. A slightly more textured, even base helps hold moisture and gives the grass roots a better chance to anchor.

Once the grass is laid, extra attention to the first watering cycle matters. The area should be kept evenly moist without becoming flooded. This helps the roots settle before the hot part of the season begins.

If the area receives strong sun or is easy to erode, a more resilient grass type may be the smarter option. Good preparation and a stable watering routine usually make the difference.
